# Therapy - Grok, Survivor Tips

**Somatic Gym Practices**

* Pre-gym grounding, sit in car for 1 to 2 mins and center myself. Hand on chest, deep breath, repeat, "This is for my body's healing"
* Embodied Weight Training - during lifts focus on the sensations rather than the reps, somatic immersion
* Post-Workout Shaking (2 Minutes): After training, find a private spot (e.g., locker room, home). Shake your arms, legs, or whole body gently, as you did before, to release adrenaline and trauma tension. Pair with a low hum to engage your vagus nerve, calming your nervous system.
* Somatic Cool-Down: End with a slow stretch (e.g., forward fold, child’s pose), noticing your body’s warmth or heartbeat. Whisper, “I’m safe here,” to reinforce safety post-assault.

**Micro-Somatic Practices for Isolation at Home**

* Morning Body Check-In (2 Minutes): Each morning, sit or stand, close your eyes, and scan your body (e.g., “Are my shoulders tight? Is my breath shallow?”). Gently sway or rock to release tension, noticing what feels alive. This builds body trust, countering loneliness.
* Tactile Anchor (Anytime): Keep a textured object (e.g., a soft scarf, a wooden bead) on your desk while WFH. Touch it when isolation or flashbacks hit, focusing on its feel to anchor you. Pair with a breath and say, “I’m here now.”
* Somatic Journaling (5 Minutes Weekly): Write a stream-of-consciousness note about how your body feels (e.g., “Chest heavy, feet grounded”). Let your hand move intuitively, not editing. This externalizes trauma somatically, reducing shame’s grip.
* Virtual Connection Prep: Before engaging online (e.g., a work call, survivor forum), shake your hands or roll your shoulders for 30 seconds to release nervous energy. This primes your body for connection, easing social fear.

## Somatic Transition Ritual to Shift from Binge-Viewing to Content Creation 

* Pre-Work Ritual (2-3 Minutes): Before starting content creation, stand and shake your arms, legs, or whole body (like your past practice) for 30-60 seconds to release hypervigilant tension. Breathe deeply and say, “This is my space to create, and it’s safe.” Follow with a tactile anchor (e.g., touch a desk item) to ground you.
* Micro-Task Start: Set a 5-minute timer to begin one tiny content task (e.g., write one tweet, outline a video idea). Focus on the physical act (e.g., typing, pen moving) to stay embodied, not the outcome. If fear spikes, pause, shake again, and resume.
* Post-Task Release: After creating, sway gently or stretch for 1 minute, saying, “I took a step, and that’s enough.” This reinforces progress somatically, reducing shame.
* Replace Viewing Time: Cap binge-viewing at a set time (e.g., 10 PM), then do the ritual to shift to 10 minutes of content work. Gradually increase this, using somatic cues to ease the transition.

## Somatic Self-Compassion to Counter Fear and Self-Doubt  

* Hand-on-Heart Anchor (1 Minute): Before posting or planning content, place a hand on your heart, breathe, and say, “My knowledge is valuable. I don’t need to be perfect.” Feel the warmth to ground the words. Do this daily to build trust in your expertise.
* Embodied Affirmation (2 Minutes): Stand in a power pose (e.g., hands on hips, like your gym stance), sway slightly, and repeat, “I survived, and my voice matters.” Move intuitively to integrate the affirmation, connecting to your 26 years of experience.
* Somatic Shame Release: If self-doubt spikes (e.g., “What value do I have?”), shake your body for 30 seconds, then rest a hand on your chest, saying, “This is trauma talking, not truth.” This releases shame somatically, freeing you to create.
* Celebrate Micro-Wins: After posting or drafting, touch a meaningful object (e.g., a gym weight, a tech gadget) and say, “I showed up.” This reinforces progress without perfectionism.

## Micro-Connections to Reduce Isolation and Test Content 

* Somatic Prep for Online Posting (1 Minute): Before posting on X, shake your hands or roll your shoulders for 30 seconds to release fight-or-flight tension. Breathe and say, “This is one step, and I’m safe.” Start with a low-stakes post (e.g., a tech tip).
* Anonymous Testing: Create an anonymous X account to share one AI or software engineering idea weekly (e.g., a vLLM insight, a Grafana trick). Focus on the act of posting, not likes, to build tolerance for exposure.
* Join Tech Communities: Engage in online tech forums (e.g., Stack Overflow, Reddit’s r/MachineLearning) or X threads about AI/defense. Comment once weekly, using somatic prep to manage anxiety. Share your expertise to feel valued.
* Survivor Connection (Optional): If ready, read one post on a survivor forum (e.g., r/CPTSD) weekly, shaking before to ground. This validates your experience, reducing shame without in-person risk.

## Somatic Sobriety and Time Management to Reclaim Leave  

* Somatic Time Boundary (1 Minute): Set a viewing cutoff (e.g., 10 PM). At that time, stand, shake for 30 seconds, and say, “I choose my future.” Shift to 10 minutes of content work (e.g., drafting a post, sketching a course idea). This breaks the “purgatory” cycle.
* Somatic Sobriety Anchor: Keep a tactile object (e.g., a gym wrist wrap, a tech gadget) on your desk. Touch it when cravings or avoidance hit, saying, “Sobriety gives me clarity.” Follow with a content task to redirect energy.
* Morning Reset (2 Minutes): Start each leave day with a body scan (lie down, notice sensations from toes to head). Set one content goal (e.g., “Write one X post”) to reclaim the day, avoiding the “fear of tomorrow.”
* Content Creation Sprint: Use your leave to do one 15-minute content sprint daily (e.g., record a 1-minute AI tip video, outline a course module). Shake before and after to stay embodied, reducing perfectionism.

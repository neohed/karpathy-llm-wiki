# Tragic Realism: A Practical Training in Spotting Hubris and Utopian Thinking

Here’s a pragmatic, self-paced **adult curriculum** built directly from the framework we’ve been discussing. It’s designed for busy people (like you) who want real-world tools to spot hubris, utopian overreach, ideological blind spots, and tragic realism in politics, media, workplaces, relationships, and their own thinking.

No exams, no essays for anyone else to read—just structured reading + short, repeatable exercises that train the mental muscle. Total time: 3–6 months at 3–5 hours/week.

### Course Title
**Tragic Realism: A Practical Training in Spotting Hubris and Utopian Thinking**

### Core Goal
Develop the habit of asking:  
“Am I (or is this idea/system/person) ignoring human limits, overestimating control, or promising perfection that reality will punish?”

### Structure
Six modules. Each has:
- Core readings (short selections, 50–150 pages total per module)
- Key concepts
- Practical exercises (do them in a private journal)
- Optional supplements (free lectures/podcasts)

### Module 1: The Ancient Alarm Bell – Hubris and Human Limits (Weeks 1–3)
**Why first?** Everything else rests on this: the Greeks gave us the clearest early-warning system.

**Readings**
- Greek myths: Icarus, Phaethon, Arachne, Niobe (any modern retelling – 20–30 pages, e.g., Edith Hamilton’s *Mythology* excerpts)
- Sophocles – *Oedipus Rex* (full play, ~80 pages; focus on the hubris scenes)
- Aristotle – *Poetics* (chapters on tragedy and hamartia/hubris, ~20 pages)

**Key takeaway**  
Hubris = crossing the mortal/divine boundary → nemesis follows.

**Exercises**
- Daily “Hubris Scan”: When you read news or argue online, note any claim that sounds like “We can finally solve X forever if only we do Y radically.”
- Personal Icarus log: Write one recent decision where you ignored a warning or over-reached. What was the quiet “nemesis” that followed?

### Module 2: Ideology as Modern Hubris – The Totalitarian Temptation (Weeks 4–6)
**Readings**
- Solzhenitsyn – *The Gulag Archipelago* (abridged edition, Vol 1 chapters 1–4 + the famous “line dividing good and evil” passage)
- Hannah Arendt – *The Origins of Totalitarianism* (excerpts: “Ideology and Terror” chapter + the “loneliness” section, ~60 pages)

**Key takeaway**  
Ideology gives evildoing its justification and turns ordinary people into perpetrators.

**Exercises**
- Ideology detector: Take any strong political/social belief you hold. Write the utopian promise it makes (“If only everyone did X, then Y would disappear”). Ask: What human limits does this ignore?
- “Line through the heart” audit: Identify one way you have justified harshness toward an “enemy” group because “the cause is just.”

### Module 3: Literary Warning Shots – Dystopias as Tragic Realism (Weeks 7–9)
**Readings** (you’ve already read most – re-read key chapters)
- Orwell – *Animal Farm* (whole) + 1984 (Part 3 + Appendix on Newspeak)
- Huxley – *Brave New World* (chapters 16–18, the Mustapha Mond dialogue)
- Optional short precursor: Yevgeny Zamyatin – *We* (first 30 pages)

**Key takeaway**  
Every utopia contains the seeds of its own dystopia because it forgets human nature.

**Exercises**
- “Which dystopia are we living in today?” – Pick a current policy or tech trend and map it to one of the three books.
- Write a one-paragraph “newspeak” version of a corporate or political slogan you hear often.

### Module 4: The Psychological Machinery – Why We Fall for It (Weeks 10–12)
**Readings**
- Daniel Kahneman – *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (Part 3: Overconfidence + chapters on planning fallacy and optimism bias)
- Carl Jung – “The Shadow” (short essay or excerpt from *Modern Man in Search of a Soul*)
- Optional: Nassim Taleb – *The Black Swan* (Prologue + Chapter 1)

**Key takeaway**  
Overconfidence bias + unacknowledged shadow = personal and collective hubris.

**Exercises**
- Confidence calibration drill (Kahneman-style): Make 10 predictions about everyday things this week (weather, stock, sports score, how long a task will take). Score accuracy at the end. Most people are wildly overconfident.
- Shadow work prompt: “The trait I most dislike in others is ________. When have I shown it myself?”

### Module 5: Tragic Realism & Personal Ascent – Living with Limits (Weeks 13–15)
**Readings**
- Viktor Frankl – *Man’s Search for Meaning* (Part 1 + the logotherapy summary)
- Marcus Aurelius – *Meditations* (Book 2 + any 10–15 pages on control, fate, humility)
- Nietzsche (short selections): “What is the meaning of ascetic ideals?” from *Genealogy of Morals* or the tragic realism passages on Thucydides

**Key takeaway**  
Meaning and freedom come from accepting limits, not denying them.

**Exercises**
- Stoic “premeditatio malorum”: Spend 5 minutes each morning imagining one thing going wrong today. How will you respond with dignity?
- Frankl log: At day’s end, note one situation where you chose your attitude despite circumstances.

### Module 6: Integration & Lifelong Practice (Weeks 16–18 + ongoing)
**Capstone Project**
1. Choose a current “hot” issue (AI regulation, culture-war topic, corporate vision statement, your own career plan).
2. Run it through the full framework:  
   - Greek lens: Where is the hubris?  
   - Ideological lens: What utopian promise is being sold?  
   - Dystopian lens: Which novel does this echo?  
   - Psychological lens: Which bias/shadow is at work?  
   - Tragic-realist lens: What limits must we accept? What small, non-utopian improvement is actually possible?

**Daily/Weekly Habits to Keep the Training Alive**
- 5-minute evening journal: “Today I spotted hubris/overconfidence in ________ (news, conversation, myself).”
- Quarterly re-read of one key passage (Solzhenitsyn’s “line” paragraph is perfect).
- “Nemesis check”: Before committing to a big goal or opinion, ask “What could realistically go wrong that I’m not admitting?”

### Resources to Make It Easy
- All core texts have cheap paperbacks, free PDFs (Project Gutenberg, archive.org), or excellent audiobooks.
- Free lectures: Yale Open Courses – “Introduction to Ancient Greek Literature” (Oedipus lectures); “The Moral Foundations of Politics” (some Arendt/Solzhenitsyn overlap).
- Podcasts: *The Partially Examined Life* episodes on these books; *Philosophize This!* on hubris & tragedy.

This isn’t academic study—it’s mental self-defence training. Do the exercises consistently and you’ll start catching the patterns in real time: in election promises, tech hype, workplace “transformational” initiatives, your own anger, or even family arguments.

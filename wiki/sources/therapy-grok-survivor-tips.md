# Therapy — Grok, Survivor Tips

## Source overview

A personalised therapeutic protocol, likely generated with AI assistance, for a trauma survivor working from home, in recovery, with significant technical and creative expertise (AI/software engineering, ~26 years of experience) that fear and shame are preventing them from sharing publicly. The document is structured as an applied somatic programme: grounding before and after effortful activity, micro-practices for isolated daily life, rituals for transitioning out of passive binge-viewing into creative work, and a concrete weekly implementation plan.

The through-line is body-first regulation: the nervous system is addressed directly through movement, breath, and touch before cognitive or behavioural change is attempted.

---

## Document structure

### 1. Somatic gym practices

Four practices anchored to the gym visit as a structured recovery window:

- **Pre-gym grounding** — 1–2 minutes sitting in the car, hand on chest, affirming "This is for my body's healing." Frames exercise as care rather than performance.
- **Embodied weight training** — attention placed on physical sensation during lifts rather than rep count. Somatic immersion as a dissociation counter.
- **Post-workout shaking** — 2 minutes of full-body shaking to release adrenaline and trauma-held tension. Paired with low humming to activate the vagus nerve.
- **Somatic cool-down** — slow stretching (child's pose, forward fold) with whispered self-affirmation: "I'm safe here."

The gym is explicitly framed as a site of healing from assault, not merely fitness.

### 2. Micro-somatic practices for home isolation

Lightweight daily practices for WFH/leave periods:

- **Morning body scan (2 mins)** — eyes closed, scan from toes to head, noting tension or breath. Gentle swaying to release. Builds body trust.
- **Tactile anchor** — a textured object kept on the desk. Touch during flashbacks or isolation spikes, paired with breath and "I'm here now." See [[concepts/tactile-anchoring]].
- **Somatic journaling (5 mins weekly)** — stream-of-consciousness writing about physical sensation (e.g., "Chest heavy, feet grounded"). Not edited. Externalises trauma somatically.
- **Virtual connection prep** — 30 seconds of shoulder rolls or hand shaking before online interactions to discharge nervous energy before engagement.

### 3. Transition ritual: passive consumption → content creation

Structured somatic bridge for breaking binge-viewing cycles:

- **Pre-work ritual** — 30–60 seconds of full-body shaking, deep breath, affirmation ("This is my space to create, and it's safe"), tactile grounding.
- **Micro-task start** — 5-minute timer on one tiny task (one tweet, one video outline). Focus on the physical act of doing, not the outcome.
- **Post-task release** — 1-minute sway or stretch, affirming "I took a step, and that's enough." Reinforces progress without perfectionism.
- **Viewing cutoff** — set time (e.g., 10 PM) acts as a hard boundary, with the transition ritual replacing passive time with a short content sprint.

This section addresses [[concepts/creative-avoidance]] directly — the cycle of hypervigilance, passive numbing, and shame about not creating.

### 4. Somatic self-compassion practices

Counteracting fear of exposure and expertise-shame:

- **Hand-on-heart anchor** — one hand on chest, warmth felt deliberately, paired with "My knowledge is valuable. I don't need to be perfect." Daily repetition to build trust in one's own expertise. See [[concepts/shame-release]].
- **Embodied affirmation** — power pose (hands on hips), gentle swaying, "I survived, and my voice matters." Movement integrates the affirmation into the body rather than leaving it cognitive.
- **Somatic shame release** — when self-doubt spikes, shake for 30 seconds, then hand on chest: "This is trauma talking, not truth."
- **Micro-win celebration** — touch a meaningful object (gym weight, tech gadget) after posting or drafting. Reinforces [[concepts/agency]] without perfectionism.

Note: document flags a need for a "sacred object" — a physical anchor not yet identified.

### 5. Micro-connection strategies

Low-risk steps for reducing isolation while managing exposure anxiety:

- **Somatic prep before posting** — 30-second hand shake or shoulder roll before publishing anything online. "This is one step, and I'm safe."
- **Anonymous X account** — post one AI/software tip weekly without identity exposure. Builds tolerance for visibility.
- **Tech community engagement** — one comment per week in a forum (Stack Overflow, r/MachineLearning, X threads). Weekly cadence, somatic prep required.
- **Survivor forum (optional)** — read one post on r/CPTSD weekly, shaking beforehand. Validation without in-person risk.

### 6. Somatic sobriety and time management

Practices for reclaiming leave days from avoidance loops:

- **Somatic time boundary** — standing, shaking, and affirming "I choose my future" at the viewing cutoff. Shift to 10 minutes of content work immediately after.
- **Somatic sobriety anchor** — tactile object on desk touched during cravings or avoidance urges, paired with "Sobriety gives me clarity." Redirects to a content task.
- **Morning reset** — body scan (lying down, toes to head) followed by setting one concrete daily goal.
- **Daily 15-minute content sprint** — record a 1-minute AI tip, outline a course module. Shake before and after.

### 7. Weekly implementation plan

Concrete schedule:

| Frequency | Practice |
|---|---|
| Daily (morning) | 2-min body scan + one content goal |
| Daily (evening) | 10 PM cutoff → shaking ritual → 5–10 min content work |
| Anytime | Hand-on-heart if shame spikes |
| Gym days (2–3×) | Pre-gym grounding, embodied lifting, post-gym idea jot, cool-down |
| Weekly | Anonymous X post, one tech forum comment, somatic journal entry |
| Daily (ongoing) | Somatic sobriety anchor during cravings/late-night urges |

## Key themes and connections

**Body as the entry point for all change.** Every practice starts with the body — shaking, breathing, touching, scanning — before addressing thought or behaviour. This reflects a somatic trauma model: the nervous system must be regulated before agency is possible. See [[concepts/nervous-system-regulation]].

**Shame as the primary barrier.** Expertise-shame — doubting the value of one's own knowledge — is named explicitly. The self-compassion practices are designed to counter the belief that decades of technical experience are worthless. See [[concepts/shame-release]] and [[concepts/shadow-integration]].

**Creative avoidance as symptom, not character flaw.** The binge-viewing cycle is framed as hypervigilance discharge and numbing, not laziness. The transition rituals treat avoidance as a nervous system state to be moved through, not a habit to be willpowered away. See [[concepts/creative-avoidance]].

**Micro-scale as the therapeutic unit.** Nothing in the implementation plan exceeds 15 minutes. One tweet. One comment. One body scan. This granularity is deliberate: perfectionism and scale-fear are named obstacles, and the programme keeps the unit of action small enough to tolerate. See [[concepts/agency]].

**Tactile anchoring as a portable resource.** The repeated use of physical objects — gym wraps, gadgets, scarves, beads — as somatic anchors is a consistent motif. The body needs something external and concrete to orient to. See [[concepts/tactile-anchoring]] and [[concepts/mindfulness]].

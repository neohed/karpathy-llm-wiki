# Tragic Realism: A Practical Training in Spotting Hubris and Utopian Thinking

A self-paced adult curriculum designed to build practical skills for spotting hubris, utopian overreach, ideological blind spots, and tragic realism across politics, media, workplaces, relationships, and one's own thinking. Six modules, 3–6 months at 3–5 hours per week. Exercises are private, journal-based, and deliberately repeatable — framed explicitly as mental self-defence training rather than academic study.

## Core Goal

> "Am I — or is this idea, system, or person — ignoring human limits, overestimating control, or promising perfection that reality will punish?"

The curriculum's purpose is to make this question a reflex: automatic, rapid, applicable in real time.

## Six-Module Structure

### Module 1 — The Ancient Alarm Bell: Hubris and Human Limits (Weeks 1–3)

Establishes the foundational pattern: [[concepts/hubris]] as boundary-crossing that inevitably attracts [[concepts/nemesis]]. The Greeks are treated as the earliest systematic early-warning system.

**Key readings:** Greek myths (Icarus, Phaethon, Arachne, Niobe); Sophocles — *Oedipus Rex*; Aristotle — *Poetics* (chapters on tragedy and [[concepts/hamartia]])

**Core exercise:** Daily "Hubris Scan" — noting any claim in news or argument that sounds like "We can finally solve X forever if only we do Y radically." Supplemented by a personal Icarus log tracking moments of personal overreach and their quiet consequences.

---

### Module 2 — Ideology as Modern Hubris: The Totalitarian Temptation (Weeks 4–6)

Extends the ancient pattern into modern political history. [[concepts/ideology-as-justification]] is the mechanism by which utopian overreach transforms ordinary people into perpetrators.

**Key readings:** [[entities/aleksandr-solzhenitsyn]] — *The Gulag Archipelago* (abridged, including the "line dividing good and evil" passage); [[entities/hannah-arendt]] — *The Origins of Totalitarianism* ("Ideology and Terror" + "loneliness" section)

**Core exercise:** Ideology detector — take any strong belief, write out its utopian promise explicitly, then identify what human limits it ignores. Supplemented by a "line through the heart" audit examining personal in-group/out-group justifications.

---

### Module 3 — Literary Warning Shots: Dystopias as Tragic Realism (Weeks 7–9)

Treats the major dystopian novels as applied tragic realism — each demonstrating how utopian premises contain the seeds of their own inversion because they forget human nature.

**Key readings:** [[entities/george-orwell]] — *Animal Farm* (whole) + *1984* (Part 3 + Newspeak appendix); [[entities/aldous-huxley]] — *Brave New World* (chapters 16–18, the Mustapha Mond dialogue); optional: [[entities/yevgeny-zamyatin]] — *We* (first 30 pages)

**Core exercise:** "Which dystopia are we living in today?" — mapping current policies or tech trends to one of the three novels. Supplemented by writing a one-paragraph "newspeak" version of a familiar corporate or political slogan.

---

### Module 4 — The Psychological Machinery: Why We Fall for It (Weeks 10–12)

Grounds utopian susceptibility in cognitive psychology and depth psychology. [[concepts/overconfidence-bias]] and the unacknowledged [[concepts/shadow-self]] are the internal mechanisms that make hubris feel like clarity.

**Key readings:** [[entities/daniel-kahneman]] — *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (Part 3: Overconfidence, planning fallacy, optimism bias); [[entities/carl-jung]] — "The Shadow" essay; optional: [[entities/nassim-taleb]] — *The Black Swan* (Prologue + Chapter 1)

**Core exercise:** Confidence calibration drill — make 10 everyday predictions, score them at week's end. Shadow work prompt: identify the trait most disliked in others and trace it back to oneself.

---

### Module 5 — Tragic Realism and Personal Ascent: Living with Limits (Weeks 13–15)

Shifts from diagnosis to praxis. [[concepts/acceptance-of-limits]] is not resignation but the precondition for genuine meaning and freedom. Stoic and existentialist frameworks are treated as operational tools.

**Key readings:** [[entities/viktor-frankl]] — *Man's Search for Meaning* (Part 1 + logotherapy summary); [[entities/marcus-aurelius]] — *Meditations* (Book 2 + selections on control, fate, humility); [[entities/friedrich-nietzsche]] — tragic realism passages on Thucydides / ascetic ideals from *Genealogy of Morals*

**Core exercise:** [[concepts/premeditatio-malorum]] — 5 minutes each morning imagining one thing going wrong and rehearsing a dignified response. Frankl log — noting at day's end one situation where attitude was chosen despite circumstances.

---

### Module 6 — Integration and Lifelong Practice (Weeks 16–18 + ongoing)

The capstone applies the full five-lens framework to a single live issue of the student's choosing. Ongoing habits are specified to keep the training active after the formal curriculum ends.

**Capstone framework — five lenses applied in sequence:**

1. **Greek lens** — Where is the hubris?
2. **Ideological lens** — What utopian promise is being sold?
3. **Dystopian lens** — Which novel does this echo?
4. **Psychological lens** — Which bias or shadow is at work?
5. **Tragic-realist lens** — What limits must be accepted? What small, non-utopian improvement is actually possible?

**Ongoing habits:** 5-minute evening journal noting spotted hubris; quarterly re-read of a key passage (Solzhenitsyn's "line" paragraph recommended); "Nemesis check" before committing to a major goal or opinion.

## Integrative Framework

The capstone five-lens structure is the document's most reusable artifact. It is not specific to the curriculum — it is a portable analytical template applicable to any claim, policy, initiative, or personal plan. The sequence matters: Greek first (pattern recognition), ideological second (promise identification), dystopian third (historical echo), psychological fourth (internal mechanism), tragic-realist fifth (constructive residue after illusions are stripped).

## Notes

The document frames this as a "mental self-defence" curriculum rather than intellectual enrichment — the exercises are deliberately practical and private, designed to train pattern recognition that fires automatically in real-time situations. The progression is intentional: ancient pattern → modern historical instantiation → literary illustration → psychological mechanism → personal practice → integration. Each module builds on the prior one rather than being independently usable.

The curriculum has strong thematic overlap with [[sources/framework-anti-ideological-thinking]], [[sources/burke-hayek-popper-triad]], and [[sources/shadow-work]], all of which address adjacent questions from different angles.

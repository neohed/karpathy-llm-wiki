# Overview of the Burke–Hayek–Popper Triad

## Abstract / Overview

A personal note grouping [[entities/edmund-burke]], [[entities/friedrich-hayek]], and [[entities/karl-popper]] as a coherent intellectual triad. Despite no direct collaboration (Burke predates the others by a century), their ideas converge on an **anti-utopian, tragic-realist vision of politics** that emphasises humility about human reason, respect for evolved institutions, and piecemeal improvement over rationalist redesign.

## Key Claims

- The triad shares four interlocking principles: [[concepts/epistemic-humility]], preference for [[concepts/evolved-institutions]], [[concepts/piecemeal-reform]], and safeguards against tyranny.
- [[entities/edmund-burke]]: Society is an intergenerational partnership; long-established institutions embody accumulated practical wisdom superior to any individual's rational blueprint.
- [[entities/friedrich-hayek]]: [[concepts/spontaneous-order]] and the [[concepts/knowledge-problem]] show why central planners cannot efficiently aggregate dispersed, tacit information; [[concepts/constructivist-rationalism]] is a "fatal conceit."
- [[entities/karl-popper]]: Politics should mirror science — [[concepts/falsification]], modest reversible experiments ([[concepts/piecemeal-social-engineering]]), and rejection of [[concepts/historicism]] and utopianism.
- Together they ask of any reform: "Do you truly understand the system you are altering, and do you have evidence your change will produce better results rather than merely different problems?"
- The framework is analogised to software engineering: *don't refactor legacy code without tests or a rollback plan.*

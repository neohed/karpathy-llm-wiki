# Edmund Burke (1729–1797)

## Overview

- Irish-born British statesman, philosopher, and Member of Parliament.
- Best known for *Reflections on the Revolution in France* (1790) — a warning against the French Revolution written during its early, seemingly moderate phase.
- Considered a founding figure of modern conservative political thought.
- Part of the [[concepts/burke-hayek-popper-triad]] anti-utopian framework.

## Key Ideas

- **Organic change vs. radical rupture**: Society is a living, intergenerational partnership between the living, the dead, and the unborn — not a blank slate for abstract redesign.
- **Prescription and prejudice**: Long-established institutions and customs embody accumulated practical wisdom superior to any individual's rational blueprint. Change should be slow and rooted in existing forms.
- **[[concepts/tragic-realism]]**: Human nature is flawed and imperfectible; politics is the art of the possible. Hubris about remaking the world leads to tyranny and terror.
- **[[concepts/little-platoons]]**: Loyalty to family, local community, and intermediate institutions protects liberty better than central power.
- Great conservative skeptic of Enlightenment rationalism when it turns revolutionary.

## Major Works

- *Reflections on the Revolution in France* (1790)

## Affiliations

- British Whig politician; later regarded as a foundational conservative thinker.

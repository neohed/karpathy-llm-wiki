# Friedrich Hayek (1899–1992)

## Overview

- Austrian-British economist and social theorist.
- Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences, 1974.
- Fled Nazism; became one of the 20th century's most influential critics of central planning.
- Part of the [[concepts/burke-hayek-popper-triad]] anti-utopian framework.

## Key Ideas

- **[[concepts/spontaneous-order]]**: Society's most valuable institutions (law, language, markets, morals) emerge through evolutionary trial-and-error, not deliberate design. They embody more knowledge than any planner can possess.
- **[[concepts/knowledge-problem]]**: Information is dispersed, local, and tacit. Central authorities cannot aggregate it efficiently; imposing "rational" order produces coercion and inefficiency.
- **[[concepts/constructivist-rationalism]]** (his great target): The delusion that evolved institutions can be scrapped and replaced with a clean logical design — the "fatal conceit" behind socialism and modern technocracy.
- **[[concepts/rule-of-law]] and limited government**: Liberty is best preserved by general, abstract rules rather than outcomes engineered by the state.
- Extends Burke's organicism into economics and social theory.

## Major Works

- *The Road to Serfdom* (1944)
- *The Constitution of Liberty* (1960)

## Affiliations

- London School of Economics; University of Chicago; University of Freiburg.

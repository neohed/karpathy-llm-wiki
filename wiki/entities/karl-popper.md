# Karl Popper (1902–1994)

## Overview

- Austrian-British philosopher of science and politics.
- Revolutionised philosophy of science; also a major defender of liberal democracy against totalitarianism.
- Wrote in exile during World War II.
- Part of the [[concepts/burke-hayek-popper-triad]] anti-utopian framework.

## Key Ideas

- **[[concepts/falsification]]**: Scientific knowledge advances by attempting to falsify theories, not by proving them true. The same critical spirit should govern politics.
- **[[concepts/piecemeal-social-engineering]]**: Reform should be modest, reversible, and testable — tackling specific ills rather than redesigning whole societies according to utopian blueprints.
- **[[concepts/historicism]]** (his target): The belief that history has an inevitable direction, or that a perfect society can be engineered, leads to authoritarianism. Chief examples: Plato, Hegel, Marx.
- **[[concepts/open-society]]**: A society that values individual freedom, institutional checks, and peaceful correction of errors through criticism and democracy. Opposed to "closed societies" that suppress criticism in the name of a final Truth.
- Supplies the scientific and methodological backbone for the triad: politics, like science, should be experimental and humble.

## Major Works

- *The Open Society and Its Enemies* (1945)
- *The Logic of Scientific Discovery* (1934/1959)

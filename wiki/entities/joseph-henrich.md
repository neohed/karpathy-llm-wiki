# Joseph Henrich

Joseph Henrich is a professor of human evolutionary biology at Harvard, best known for synthesising cultural evolution, cognitive anthropology, and behavioural economics into a unified account of how human psychology and institutions co-evolve. His empirical work provides the firmest scientific grounding for the anti-ideological thinking framework described in [[sources/framework-anti-ideological-thinking]].

---

## Core thesis: WEIRD psychology as the outlier

Henrich's most consequential contribution is the WEIRD critique. Western, Educated, Industrialised, Rich, and Democratic populations — the people who have historically supplied most psychology research subjects — turn out to be statistical outliers on a wide range of cognitive and behavioural measures. The globe's default human psychology is far more collectivist, context-dependent, and norm-governed than the WEIRD baseline assumes.

The implication is radical: much of what Western intellectuals mistake for universal human rationality or universal moral intuition is actually a local cultural product, shaped by specific historical institutions (the medieval Catholic Church's marriage and inheritance rules being a key driver of WEIRD psychology in Henrich's account). Claims to universal ideology are therefore doubly suspect — they mistake one cultural variant for the whole.

---

## Cultural evolution as the core mechanism

Henrich's theoretical framework treats culture as an evolutionary system: practices, norms, and cognitive tools spread through populations via imitation, prestige-biasing, and selection. What survives is not necessarily what is true or morally optimal, but what scales cooperation and reduces coordination costs in a given ecological and social environment.

Key implications for the anti-ideological toolkit:

- **Norms encode accumulated problem-solving.** When a cultural norm appears irrational or arbitrary, the first question is not "is this wrong?" but "what selection pressures favoured this, and in what environment?" This is the [[concepts/steel-manning]] move applied at civilisational scale.
- **Psychological tools are cultural achievements.** Rewarding nuance over certainty, tolerating cognitive dissonance, practising [[concepts/epistemic-humility]] — these are not natural defaults. They emerged and spread because they improved collective sense-making in complex, high-information environments. They can be taught and reinforced.
- **Cumulative culture outperforms individual cognition.** No single mind, however brilliant, can reverse-engineer what millennia of cultural evolution have encoded. This grounds a deep conservatism about utopian rewrites — see [[concepts/utopian-thinking]] — while remaining fully compatible with iterative, evidence-based reform.

---

## Henrich and systems thinking

Henrich's methodology is inherently systems-oriented. He maps social outcomes as emergent properties of interacting variables — kinship structures, religious institutions, market integration, literacy rates — operating across long time horizons with feedback loops that are invisible at the individual level. This is exactly the approach advocated in [[concepts/systems-thinking]]: treating any social or historical question as a complex adaptive system rather than a morality narrative.

His work on the Church, kin networks, and WEIRD psychology is a master class in tracing positive and negative feedback loops across centuries without reaching for a simple villain or hero.

---

## Henrich and falsifiability

Henrich is unusual among cultural theorists in his commitment to cross-cultural quantitative data and pre-specified predictions. His claims are falsifiable and have repeatedly been tested against data from small-scale societies, historical natural experiments, and economic games run across dozens of cultures. This aligns him firmly with the Popperian epistemology that anchors the anti-ideological framework — see [[concepts/falsifiability]] and [[entities/karl-popper]].

## Relationship to the anti-ideological framework

In [[sources/framework-anti-ideological-thinking]], Henrich's work is cited as empirical grounding for the claim that nuance-rewarding cognitive practices are evolutionary achievements, not arbitrary preferences. Specifically:

- The WEIRD critique undermines any ideology that claims to speak for universal human nature.
- The cultural-evolution lens provides the steel-manning question: "What selection pressures favoured this norm in that society?" — making it possible to understand rather than merely judge.
- His emphasis on cumulative cultural learning supports the incremental, refactoring approach to belief revision over utopian cognitive overhaul.

## Key works

- *The WEIRDest People in the World* (2020) — full account of WEIRD psychology origins
- *The Secret of Our Success* (2015) — cultural evolution as the source of human cognitive advantage
- Cross-cultural behavioural economics research with colleagues including Robert Boyd and Herbert Gintis

# Habit Tracking

## Overview

The practice of visually recording habit completion — via calendars, journals, apps, paper clips, or other tools — to reinforce the **4th Law of Behavior Change: Make It Satisfying**. Covered in [[entities/james-clear]]'s *Atomic Habits* (Chapter 16).

## Why It Works

Tracking simultaneously reinforces multiple laws:
- **Obvious**: makes progress visible and patterns clear.
- **Attractive**: creates a streak worth maintaining.
- **Satisfying**: recording a successful completion is immediately rewarding.

## Key Benefits

- See patterns clearly (streaks on a calendar become motivating in themselves).
- Stay honest about actual behavior vs. perceived behavior.
- Feel rewarded every time you record success.
- Build motivation by showing proof of progress on tough days.
- Reinforce identity — each mark is a vote for who you're becoming.

## Key Rules

- **"Don't break the chain"**: strive to maintain streaks.
- **"Never miss twice"**: missing once is an accident; missing twice starts a new bad habit. Recovery speed matters more than perfection.
- Small actions on bad days (just showing up) preserve momentum and identity.

## Cautions

- **[[concepts/goodharts-law]]**: if the measure becomes the target, you risk optimizing for the metric at the expense of the real outcome.
- Numbers don't capture everything — qualitative progress (energy, mood, confidence) also matters.
- Track only your most important habits; avoid tracking overload.

## Implementation

- Automate where possible (devices, apps).
- Use [[concepts/habit-stacking]]: link tracking to the habit itself (*"After I [habit], I will record it."*).
- Tracking isn't permanent — even a short measurement period can be highly insightful.

# Noble Eightfold Path

## Overview

The Noble Eightfold Path (Pali: *Ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo*) is the central practical teaching of Buddhism — the path to the cessation of suffering (dukkha) and to liberation (nibbana). It is one of the core elements of the Four Noble Truths.

## The Eight Components

Grouped into three categories:

### Wisdom (Pañña)
1. **Right View (Samma Ditthi)** — understanding the nature of reality, impermanence, and suffering.
2. **Right Intention (Samma Sankappa)** — motivations of goodwill, compassion, and non-harm. See [[concepts/right-intention]].

### Ethical Conduct (Sila)
3. **Right Speech (Samma Vaca)** — truthful, kind, constructive communication. See [[concepts/right-speech]].
4. **Right Action (Samma Kammanta)** — ethical behavior; avoiding harm, theft, sexual misconduct.
5. **Right Livelihood (Samma Ajiva)** — earning a living without causing harm.

### Mental Cultivation (Samadhi)
6. **Right Effort (Samma Vayama)** — cultivating wholesome states; abandoning unwholesome ones.
7. **Right Mindfulness (Samma Sati)** — present-moment awareness. See [[concepts/mindfulness]].
8. **Right Concentration (Samma Samadhi)** — meditative absorption and mental unification.

## Core Purpose

- Provides a comprehensive framework for ethical living, mental development, and wisdom.
- Applies to both lay practitioners and monastics.
- Practical path: not merely philosophical, but a structured set of practices.

# Tragic Realism

## Overview

The political-philosophical stance, associated especially with [[entities/edmund-burke]], that **human nature is flawed and imperfectible**, and that politics is therefore the art of the possible rather than the pursuit of perfection.

## Key Points

- Hubris — the belief that one generation can remake the world anew — historically leads to tyranny and terror (e.g., the French Revolution's Reign of Terror).
- A system's value is measured not by how optimal it looks in theory but by **how well it prevents catastrophic failure**: cronyism, totalitarianism, cultural erosion.
- Aligns with [[concepts/epistemic-humility]] and the broader [[concepts/burke-hayek-popper-triad]] skepticism of utopian redesign.
- Distinguishes conservative skepticism from mere nostalgia: it is a positive program for **[[concepts/ordered-liberty]]**.

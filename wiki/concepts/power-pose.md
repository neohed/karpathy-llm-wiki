# Power Pose

## Overview

A body posture (e.g., hands on hips, arms wide, upright stance) adopted intentionally to influence internal psychological state — increasing feelings of confidence, competence, and safety. Used in [[sources/therapy-grok-survivor-tips]] as part of [[concepts/embodied-affirmation]] practice.

## Usage in Somatic Context

- Stand in a power pose while repeating affirmations to integrate them physically.
- The posture signals safety and agency to the nervous system before the cognitive content is fully believed.
- Particularly useful for [[concepts/cptsd]] recovery, where the body habitually contracts under threat.

## Note on Evidence

- The original "power posing" research (Cuddy et al.) faced replication challenges regarding hormonal effects, but the behavioral and psychological priming effects remain widely used in therapeutic and coaching contexts.

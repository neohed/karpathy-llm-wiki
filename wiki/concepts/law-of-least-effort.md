# Law of Least Effort

## Overview

A principle from [[entities/james-clear]]'s *Atomic Habits* (Chapter 12) stating that **when faced with multiple options, people naturally gravitate toward the path requiring the least energy**. This is an evolutionary efficiency, not a character flaw.

## Core Insight

- **Motivation is overrated; friction is what matters most.**
- Instead of relying on willpower, reduce the energy cost of good habits and increase it for bad habits.

## Practical Applications

### Reducing Friction (Good Habits)
- Design environment so desired behavior is the default or most convenient option.
- Place cues along existing daily paths (gym on the way to work).
- **Prime the environment**: lay out gym clothes, prep food, arrange creative tools in advance.
- **Reset the room**: restore spaces to their intended state after use.

### Adding Friction (Bad Habits)
- Add small barriers between yourself and undesired behaviors (unplug TV, delete apps, remove batteries from remote).
- Use environmental cues to force conscious choice (say the show's name before plugging in the TV).

## Key Question

> *"How can I design my environment so that the right action is the easiest action?"*

## Cumulative Advantage

- Small, consistent friction reductions compound over time into a lifestyle where good choices require less thought and bad ones require more effort.

# Identity-Based Habits

## Overview

A framework from [[entities/james-clear]]'s *Atomic Habits* proposing that **lasting behavior change requires identity change**, not just outcome or process change. Rather than pursuing a goal, you become the type of person for whom the desired behavior is natural.

## Three Levels of Change

1. **Outcomes** — what you get (e.g., lose 10 kg)
2. **Processes** — what you do (e.g., go to the gym)
3. **Identity** — what you believe (e.g., "I am a healthy person")

Most people work outside-in (outcome → process). Lasting change works inside-out (identity → process → outcome).

## Key Points

- Every action is a vote for your desired identity; repeated votes shift self-image.
- Identity is not fixed or innate — it is constructed through repeated behavior.
- Bad habits reinforce negative identity loops ("I'm not a morning person", "I'm bad at math").
- Challenging those beliefs with new behaviors is the mechanism of identity change.

## Two-Step Process

1. Decide who you want to be.
2. Prove it to yourself with small wins.

## Practical Heuristic

> *"What would a [healthy/creative/disciplined] person do right now?"*

### From [[sources/atomic-habits-part-03]] (2026-05-07)
- **Two-Minute Rule reinforces identity (Ch. 13)**: even two minutes of effort casts a vote for the kind of person you want to become. Consistency of identity votes matters more than magnitude of individual actions.
- **Habit tracking as identity reinforcement (Ch. 16)**: each mark on a tracker is a vote for who you're becoming — not just a record of behavior.
- **Group identity (Ch. 9)**: "We are readers" sustains habits longer than "I am a reader." Social immersion in a group that embodies desired identity is one of the most powerful habit-design strategies.
- **Flexible identity (Ch. 20)**: too rigid an identity (defined by roles rather than traits) resists change and hinders growth. Define identity by values and traits to remain adaptable when circumstances shift.
- **Identity as the ultimate reward (Ch. 15)**: over time, the sense of being a certain kind of person becomes the primary source of satisfaction — more durable than external rewards.

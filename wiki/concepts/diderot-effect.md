# Diderot Effect

## Overview

Originally an observation about consumption behavior — named after philosopher Denis Diderot — describing how **one acquisition or change triggers a cascade of further changes** to maintain coherence or identity. Applied in [[entities/james-clear]]'s *Atomic Habits* (Chapter 5) to habit formation.

## Original Concept

- Diderot received a new scarlet robe as a gift; its elegance made his other possessions feel shabby, triggering a chain of new purchases to match.
- Illustrates how a single change can set off a self-reinforcing spiral.

## Application to Habits

- A single new habit can cascade into a chain of connected good habits — *routine momentum*.
- This is the engine behind [[concepts/habit-stacking]]: each habit naturally triggers the next.
- The effect can be negative (one bad habit enabling others) or deliberately designed to be positive.

## Key Points

- Recognizing the Diderot Effect allows deliberate design of habit chains.
- Pair with [[concepts/habit-stacking]] to harness positive cascades intentionally.

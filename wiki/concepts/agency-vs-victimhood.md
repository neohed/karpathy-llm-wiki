# Agency vs. Victimhood

## Overview

The distinction between taking personal responsibility for one's circumstances (agency) and attributing outcomes to external forces while expecting others to rectify them (victimhood). A central pillar of [[entities/jimmy-carr]]'s life philosophy.

## Key Points

- **Agency**: placing yourself in charge of your own outcomes; acting rather than waiting.
- **Victimhood**: a psychologically tempting stance that yields social sympathy but erodes capability and drive.
- "Excellence is hard, victimhood is easy." — the path of least resistance leads to victimhood.
- Entitlement = wanting a better life + expecting someone else to create it. Real ambition closes that gap with action.
- Related to [[concepts/epistemic-humility]]: acknowledging your own role in outcomes without excessive self-blame or self-excuse.

## Relation to Other Concepts

- Complements [[concepts/identity-based-habits]]: choosing an agent identity over a victim identity shapes all downstream behaviour.
- Resonates with [[concepts/piecemeal-social-engineering]] and [[concepts/tragic-realism]]: life involves real unfairness; the stoic response is to act within constraints rather than wait for perfect conditions.
- Contrasts with [[concepts/historicism]]: ideologies that cast social groups as inevitable victims of historical forces can entrench victimhood narratives.

# Cultural Evolution

## Overview

The study of how cultural practices, norms, beliefs, and institutions **evolve through selection processes** analogous (but not identical) to biological evolution. Associated primarily with [[entities/joseph-henrich]] and colleagues.

## Key Points

- Cultural norms and institutions that persist and spread do so because they solved real adaptive problems — even if participants can't articulate why.
- Certain psychological tools (nuance-rewarding, dissonance-tolerating) scaled human cooperation precisely because they outperformed dogmatic certainty.
- WEIRD psychology (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic) is an outlier on many psychological dimensions — a product of specific cultural evolution (e.g., medieval Church marriage rules).
- Provides a non-moralizing frame for asking: "What selection pressures favored this norm in that society, and what does it predict?"

## Relation to Anti-Ideological Thinking

- Validates [[concepts/evolved-institutions]]: norms that survive across generations likely encode adaptive information we can't fully articulate.
- Suggests [[concepts/epistemic-humility]] about the pace of change: rapid destruction of evolved norms risks losing embedded social knowledge.
- Grounds [[concepts/systems-thinking]] historically: trace feedback loops across generations, not just years.

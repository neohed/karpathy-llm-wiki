# Falsification

## Overview

The demarcation criterion and epistemological method proposed by [[entities/karl-popper]]: a claim is **scientific** if and only if it could in principle be shown to be false by empirical evidence. Knowledge advances by surviving attempts at refutation, not by accumulating confirmations.

## Key Points

- Science progresses through bold conjectures and rigorous attempts to refute them.
- No finite set of observations can prove a universal theory true; a single counter-example can prove it false.
- Popper extends this to politics: good governance is experimental and open to revision when evidence shows failure.
- Underpins [[concepts/piecemeal-social-engineering]]: design reforms to be testable and reversible.
- Connects to [[concepts/epistemic-humility]]: we can never be certain we are right, only that we haven't yet been proven wrong.

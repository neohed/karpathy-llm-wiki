# Knowledge Problem

## Overview

Formulated by [[entities/friedrich-hayek]]: the observation that economically and socially relevant information is **dispersed, local, and tacit** across millions of individuals — and therefore cannot be efficiently aggregated by any central authority.

## Key Points

- Central planners inevitably lack the information needed to make efficient decisions for complex societies.
- Attempting to impose a "rational" order on the basis of centrally held knowledge produces coercion and inefficiency.
- Price signals in markets serve as a mechanism for aggregating dispersed knowledge without central coordination.
- Underpins the critique of socialism and technocracy within the [[concepts/burke-hayek-popper-triad]] framework.

# Implementation Intentions

## Overview

A habit-formation strategy introduced in [[entities/james-clear]]'s *Atomic Habits* (Chapter 5), grounded in psychological research. An implementation intention is a **specific, pre-committed plan** stating when and where a behavior will occur.

## Format

> *"I will [BEHAVIOR] at [TIME] in [LOCATION]."*

## Key Points

- Research shows people using implementation intentions are **2–3× more likely** to follow through on a behavior than those with vague intentions.
- Specificity converts abstract goals into concrete cues — operationalizing the **1st Law: Make It Obvious**.
- Eliminates in-the-moment decision-making by pre-deciding the cue, time, and place.
- Works by anchoring the new behavior to a defined context, making the cue obvious and unambiguous.

## Example

- Vague: "I will exercise more."
- Implementation intention: "I will run for 20 minutes at 7:00 AM in the park near my house."

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- Often combined with [[concepts/habit-stacking]] for chained sequences.
- Both are practical expressions of the [[concepts/four-laws-of-behavior-change]] (1st Law).
- Reduces reliance on motivation by making the cue automatic.

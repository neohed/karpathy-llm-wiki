# Steel-Manning

## Overview

The practice of **articulating the strongest possible version of an opposing view** before evaluating or critiquing it. The inverse of a straw man.

## Key Points

- Forces genuine engagement with the best arguments on the other side, not caricatures.
- Combined with [[concepts/falsification]]: after steel-manning the opponent, turn Popper's method on your *own* position — "What evidence would make me update?"
- Reduces motivated reasoning and the confirmation bias that ideological consumption amplifies.
- Henrich-style extension: ask what selection pressures or social context made the opposing norm adaptive — "why did people believe this, and under what conditions was it reasonable?"

## Practice

1. Read two opposing sources on any issue.
2. Write a one-paragraph steel-man of *each*.
3. Only then ask: which has stronger evidence and fewer unfalsifiable assumptions?

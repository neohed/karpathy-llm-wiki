# Steel-Manning

The practice of articulating the **strongest possible version** of an opposing view before subjecting one's own position to scrutiny. The name is a deliberate inversion of the straw man fallacy: where straw-manning caricatures an argument to make it easier to dismiss, steel-manning reconstructs it in its most coherent, well-evidenced, and charitably interpreted form.

Steel-manning is not devil's advocacy performed for rhetorical effect. It is a discipline of genuine comprehension — the goal is to understand a position well enough that its actual proponents would recognise it as a fair representation, even if they would still contest the conclusions you draw from it.

---

## The straw man contrast

| Straw man | Steel man |
|-----------|-----------|
| Picks the weakest, most extreme representative of a view | Seeks the most sophisticated, well-evidenced version |
| Optimises for easy refutation | Optimises for accurate understanding |
| Signals confidence; conceals ignorance | Exposes the limits of one's own model |
| Common in adversarial discourse | Necessary for genuine inquiry |

Straw-manning is cognitively cheap and emotionally satisfying. The brain's demand for certainty — and for social belonging with an in-group — rewards it. Steel-manning is effortful precisely because it holds that reward off until understanding is more complete.

## Connection to falsifiability

Steel-manning pairs naturally with [[concepts/falsifiability]] as developed by [[entities/karl-popper]]. The standard Popperian question is: *what evidence would falsify my own view?* Steel-manning adds the complementary question: *what is the strongest evidence and reasoning that supports the opposing view?*

Together they form a two-sided epistemic test:
1. What would make **my position** wrong? (Popperian self-scrutiny)
2. What makes the **opposing position** as right as it can be? (Steel-manning)

A belief that survives both questions is more robust than one that has merely not been challenged. A belief that fails either question should prompt revision, not rationalisation.

## The Henrich extension: evolutionary steel-manning

[[entities/joseph-henrich]]'s cultural-evolution framework suggests a further move: before evaluating whether a cultural norm, institution, or practice is *right* or *wrong*, ask what **selection pressures** might have favoured it in the society where it arose.

This is steel-manning applied to social history. Practices that appear irrational from a modern Western vantage point — kinship taboos, ritual observances, costly initiation rites — often encode accumulated adaptive logic that the culture could not articulate explicitly but that scaled cooperation or reduced free-riding. Dismissing them without asking the evolutionary question is a sophisticated form of straw-manning: substituting contemporary moral intuitions for genuine causal analysis.

The practical prompt: *"What problem was this norm solving, and for whom, under what conditions?"* This does not imply that old norms are correct, only that they deserve the same epistemic generosity extended to any other argument before being revised or discarded.

## Connection to cognitive dissonance tolerance

Genuine steel-manning produces cognitive dissonance. If the opposing view is genuinely strong, sitting with it is uncomfortable. The urge to resolve that discomfort by retreating to a preferred conclusion — what the [[sources/framework-anti-ideological-thinking]] calls the "narrative default" — is exactly what steel-manning is designed to interrupt.

The practice treats discomfort not as a signal to stop, but as a signal that understanding is deepening. The engineering analogy from the anti-ideological framework is apt: legacy code that *shouldn't* work but does is uncomfortable to confront. The productive response is to understand why it works before touching it, not to rewrite it from first principles and break production.

A useful exercise: after reading two opposing sources on any issue, write a single paragraph that steel-mans *both* without reaching a verdict. The inability to write that paragraph cleanly reveals where comprehension is thinnest.

## Place in the anti-ideological framework

Within the broader [[sources/framework-anti-ideological-thinking]], steel-manning is one of the highest-leverage practices because it directly addresses the most common failure mode: **process capture by narrative**.

Ideology provides pre-built steel men for approved positions and pre-built straw men for disapproved ones. It removes the cognitive labour of construction. The antidote is not a counter-ideology but a habit of manual reconstruction — insisting on building both the strongest opposing case and the falsification conditions for one's own, every time, before reaching conclusions.

The deliverable is not neutrality. One is permitted to reach a verdict. The requirement is that the verdict survive honest engagement with the best available counter-case.

---

## Practice prompts

- *"What would a thoughtful, well-informed proponent of the opposing view say about my argument?"*
- *"What evidence or reasoning is on their side that I have not yet accounted for?"*
- *"What selection pressures or historical conditions might have made this position adaptive for those who held it?"* (Henrich)
- *"What would I need to see to update toward their position?"* (Popper)
- *"Can I write their strongest case in a way they would recognise as fair?"*

# Two-Minute Rule

## Overview

A habit-formation strategy from [[entities/james-clear]]'s *Atomic Habits* (Chapter 13): **when starting a new habit, it should take less than two minutes to do.** The goal is to make beginning frictionless and remove the mental resistance that causes procrastination.

## Core Principle

> *"When you start a new habit, it should take less than two minutes to do."*

## Gateway Habits

Break any goal down to a tiny starting action:

| Goal | Two-Minute Version |
|---|---|
| Read more | Read one page |
| Get fit | Put on running shoes |
| Study harder | Open notebook |
| Meditate | Sit and breathe for two minutes |

## Key Points

- **Standardize before you optimize**: don't improve a habit you haven't consistently started.
- It's acceptable to *only* do the two-minute version — consistency and identity votes matter more than session length.
- **Ritualizing the beginning** cues the brain into a state of flow or focus.
- Stop while it still feels effortless — staying below the friction threshold keeps the habit sustainable.
- Related to **decisive moments**: tiny initial choices set the trajectory for what follows.

## Habit Shaping

Once the two-minute habit is solid, extend gradually:
- Phase 1: Change into gym clothes.
- Phase 2: Walk 5 minutes.
- Phase 3: Go to the gym and leave after 5 minutes.
- Phase 4: Work out for 15 minutes.
- Phase 5: Work out 3× per week.

## Connection to Identity

Each two-minute repetition casts a vote for the identity you're building — see [[concepts/identity-based-habits]].

### From [[sources/therapy-grok-survivor-tips]] (2026-05-07)
- This source applies a parallel principle under the name **"micro-task start"**: set a 5-minute timer to begin one tiny content task (write one tweet, outline a video idea).
- The focus is explicitly on **the physical act** (typing, pen moving) rather than the outcome — a somatic grounding layer added to the frictionless-start principle.
- If fear spikes mid-task: pause, shake to regulate, and resume. The two-minute-equivalent task is treated as interruptible without failure.
- The **"post-task release"** (sway/stretch + affirmation) adds a somatic bookend that the original Atomic Habits framing lacks — especially relevant where shame or anxiety is high.

# Goodhart's Law

## Overview

Originally formulated by economist Charles Goodhart; commonly stated as: **"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."**

## Application in Atomic Habits

Cited by [[entities/james-clear]] (Chapter 16) as a caution for [[concepts/habit-tracking]]: if you optimize for the metric (e.g., maintaining a streak), you may compromise the underlying goal (e.g., genuine workout quality or learning).

## Examples

- Doing a token 30-second workout to preserve a streak, without actually exercising meaningfully.
- Counting pages read per day while skimming without comprehension.
- Logging meals to hit a calorie number while ignoring nutritional quality.

## Mitigation

- Supplement quantitative tracking with qualitative reflection.
- Periodically ask: *Am I actually improving, or just gaming the metric?*
- See [[concepts/deliberate-practice]] — feedback and conscious adjustment guard against mindless repetition.

# Constructivist Rationalism

## Overview

[[entities/friedrich-hayek]]'s term for the "dangerous delusion" that evolved institutions can be **scrapped and replaced with a clean, logical design** based purely on rational principles. He called it the "fatal conceit."

## Key Points

- Treats society as a machine that can be rationally engineered from first principles.
- Ignores the [[concepts/knowledge-problem]] — the impossibility of centrally aggregating dispersed, tacit knowledge.
- Destroys [[concepts/spontaneous-order]] and the embedded wisdom of [[concepts/evolved-institutions]].
- Hayek identifies it as the intellectual error underlying socialism, central planning, and much modern technocracy.
- Related to [[entities/karl-popper]]'s critique of [[concepts/historicism]] and utopian blueprints.

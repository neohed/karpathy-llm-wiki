# Spontaneous Order

## Overview

A concept associated primarily with [[entities/friedrich-hayek]]: many of society's most valuable institutions — law, language, markets, moral norms — are **not deliberately designed** but emerge through evolutionary trial-and-error across many interactions.

## Key Points

- Spontaneous orders embody more distributed knowledge than any central planner can possess.
- They are the product of human action but not of human design.
- Attempting to replace them with rationally constructed alternatives ([[concepts/constructivist-rationalism]]) typically destroys embedded information and produces worse outcomes.
- Related to [[entities/edmund-burke]]'s concept of prescription — long-established institutions encode accumulated practical wisdom.

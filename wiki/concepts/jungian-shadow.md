# Jungian Shadow

## Overview

A concept from [[entities/carl-jung]]'s analytical psychology referring to the **disowned, repressed, or unconscious dimension of the psyche** — the parts of oneself that have been denied, hidden, or pushed underground, often beginning in childhood in response to social and family conditioning.

## Key Properties

- The shadow is not merely "the bad" — Jung held it contains **"90% pure gold"**: unlived potential, creative energy, authentic drives, and vital force.
- Suppressing the shadow doesn't eliminate it; it **burrows underground and operates autonomously**, often running one's life from below conscious awareness.
- In sensitive, self-monitoring people, shadow suppression tends to **implode inward** rather than explode outward, manifesting as [[concepts/burnout]], [[concepts/addiction]], and [[concepts/depression]].

## Shadow vs. Persona

- The **[[concepts/persona]]** is the social mask; the shadow is everything the persona hides.
- A very polished persona often signals a large, neglected shadow.

## Common Shadow Manifestations

- Jealousy, rage, resentment, lust, shame spirals, compulsive criticism, fantasies
- Burnout from self-betrayal
- Addiction as pain-management
- Depression as protest against an unlived life

## Relation to Christianity

- Traditional Western Christianity tends to frame the shadow as **"the sinful nature"** — to be fought, denied, confessed, cleansed, and crucified. This establishes no *relationship* with the shadow and leaves it unintegrated.

# Habit Loop

## Overview

The universal four-stage feedback cycle through which all habits form and operate, as described by [[entities/james-clear]] in *Atomic Habits*. Rooted in behavioral psychology and evolutionary neuroscience.

## The Four Stages

1. **Cue** — A trigger that signals the possibility of a reward. Cues become meaningful through repeated association; one person's trigger is another's background noise.
2. **Craving** — The motivational desire for a *change in state* (not for the habit action itself). The emotional engine of the loop.
3. **Response** — The actual behavior or thought performed to satisfy the craving.
4. **Reward** — Satisfies the craving and teaches the brain which behaviors are worth repeating.

## Problem Phase vs. Solution Phase

- **Problem phase**: Cue + Craving (you sense a need for change)
- **Solution phase**: Response + Reward (you act and achieve it)

## Key Properties

- The loop runs automatically once established; it underlies nearly every action.
- If any stage is weak or missing, the habit fails to form or persist.
- The brain builds habits to reduce cognitive load — automating routine decisions preserves mental energy.

## Design Implications

- See [[concepts/four-laws-of-behavior-change]] for how to engineer each stage.
- To build habits: strengthen each stage. To break habits: disrupt one or more stages.

### From [[sources/atomic-habits-part-02]] (2026-05-07)
- The **cue stage** is elaborated: cues are predominantly visual; the entire environmental context (not just a single signal) becomes the cue over time.
- Even subliminal/unconscious exposure to a cue can trigger cravings before conscious awareness registers them.
- Habits leave lasting mental imprints and can resurface when old cues re-appear, even after years of dormancy — they are disarmed, not erased.



### From [[sources/atomic-habits-part-03]] (2026-05-07)
- The **craving stage** is elaborated: desire arises from a *perceived gap* between current and desired state; the craving is not for the habit itself but for the change in state it produces (Ch. 10).
- The **reward stage** is elaborated: the brain is wired for immediate-return environments; the 4th Law exists specifically to bridge the gap between delayed real-world rewards and the brain's need for immediate satisfaction (Ch. 15).
- Appendix insight: **feelings come before *and* after action** — craving motivates the behavior; reward reinforces it. Initiation and repetition are driven by different forces (craving/desire starts; pleasure/satisfaction sustains).
- **Hope fuels new behaviors; experience grounds them**: at first, unknown outcomes create hope; with repetition, outcomes become predictable and hope gives way to realism.

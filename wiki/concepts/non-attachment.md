# Non-Attachment (Anatta / Anicca)

## Overview

Two related Buddhist doctrines that together underpin the teaching of non-attachment:

- **Anatta** (non-self): there is no fixed, permanent self — what we call "I" is a constantly changing process.
- **Anicca** (impermanence): all phenomena — thoughts, feelings, relationships, objects — are transient.

Non-attachment is the practical application: **letting go of clinging** to possessions, outcomes, status, identities, or people, recognizing that clinging to impermanent things is a source of suffering (dukkha).

## Key Points

- Non-attachment ≠ indifference; it means engaging fully without *clinging*.
- Acceptance of impermanence allows greater equanimity when facing loss or change.
- Reduces stress and anxiety by releasing the need to control outcomes.
- Supports flexible identity — a parallel to [[concepts/identity-based-habits]]' recommendation to define identity by traits, not fixed roles.
- Encourages sustainable, mindful consumption by reducing material craving.

## Application

- **Conflict resolution**: letting go of rigid attachment to specific outcomes opens space for creative solutions.
- **Personal growth**: acceptance of impermanence enables adapting to change without crisis.
- **Mindful consumption**: non-attachment to possessions supports reduced environmental impact.

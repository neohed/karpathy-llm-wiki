# Burke–Hayek–Popper Triad

## Overview

An informal grouping of [[entities/edmund-burke]], [[entities/friedrich-hayek]], and [[entities/karl-popper]] whose ideas converge on a **tragic-realist, anti-utopian framework** for politics — despite no direct collaboration and Burke predating the others by over a century.

## Four Interlocking Principles

1. **[[concepts/epistemic-humility]]** — No single mind or generation possesses enough knowledge to plan society optimally (Burke's accumulated wisdom, Hayek's [[concepts/knowledge-problem]], Popper's [[concepts/falsification]]).
2. **Preference for [[concepts/evolved-institutions]] over designed ones** — Respect what has survived the test of time unless there is strong, testable evidence a change will improve matters.
3. **[[concepts/piecemeal-reform]]** — Radical leaps are dangerous. Change should be incremental, experimental, and open to reversal.
4. **Safeguards against tyranny** — Because human nature is flawed, robust checks (tradition, [[concepts/rule-of-law]], open criticism, intermediate institutions) matter more than optimality on paper.

## Core Diagnostic Question

> "Do you truly understand the system you are altering, and do you have evidence that your change will produce better results rather than merely different problems?"

## Software Engineering Analogy

The triad's political philosophy maps onto engineering epistemology:
> *Don't refactor legacy code without tests or a rollback plan.*

## Applications

- Critique of the French Revolution, Soviet central planning, technocracy.
- Scepticism of constitutional "modernisation" projects (e.g., House of Lords reform) that replace messy but functional evolved safeguards with cleaner but more fragile designs.

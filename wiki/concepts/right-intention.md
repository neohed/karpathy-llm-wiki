# Right Intention (Samma Sankappa)

Right Intention — *Samma Sankappa* in Pali — is the second component of the Noble Eightfold Path and the ethical engine behind all action. Where [[concepts/right-speech]] governs what we say, Right Intention governs the motivational substrate from which speech and action arise. It is less a rule about behaviour than a practice of continually examining and purifying the orientation of the mind before it expresses itself in the world.

## The Three Intentions

Buddhist teaching identifies three specific intentions that constitute the "right" form:

**1. Goodwill (Nekkhamma / Metta-intention)**
The intention to act from kindness and benevolence rather than craving or self-interest. This is sometimes framed as the intention of renunciation — letting go of the pull toward personal gain as the primary motive. It does not mean self-denial, but rather that others' well-being is held genuinely, not instrumentally.

**2. Compassion (Karuna-intention)**
The intention to recognise suffering — in others and in oneself — and to be moved to alleviate it. This is not sentimental sympathy but an active orientation: decisions are filtered through the question *does this reduce suffering, or does it compound it?* See [[concepts/compassion]] for the broader treatment.

**3. Non-harm (Ahimsa-intention)**
The intention to avoid causing harm or suffering through action, speech, or indirect consequence. This extends beyond the obvious (physical violence) to subtler forms: manipulation, exploitation, deliberate humiliation, negligent disregard.

Together these three form a coherent motivational stance — not a checklist, but a direction of the will.

## Role in the Eightfold Path

Right Intention is paired with Right View (the first path factor) to constitute the *wisdom* division of the Eightfold Path. This positioning is significant: intention is placed before the ethical and practice factors (Right Speech, Right Action, Right Livelihood, Right Effort, Right Mindfulness, Right Concentration). The implication is that correct behaviour without examined intention is fragile. It may hold under normal conditions and collapse under pressure, because its roots are in habit or social expectation rather than genuine understanding.

Right Intention is also the bridge between intellectual understanding and lived ethics. One may understand impermanence (*anicca*) and non-self (*anatta*) as concepts; Right Intention is what makes that understanding operational in moment-to-moment choice.

## Unexamined Intention and Shadow

A direct implication of this framework is that *unexamined* intention is not neutral — it is driven by whatever unconscious material has not been brought into awareness. In contemporary psychological language, this is the domain of [[concepts/shadow-integration]]: the motivations we act from without knowing we act from them.

The shadow of charitable action may be the need for approval. The shadow of firm boundaries may be disguised aggression. The shadow of Right Speech, pursued without Right Intention, may be the performance of virtue for social reward.

The Buddhist prescription is not psychoanalysis, but the mechanism is structurally similar: bring intention into the light of awareness so that it can be consciously shaped rather than unconsciously enacted. Meditation practice is the primary method — creating sufficient stillness to observe the motivational layer before it becomes action.

This is why Right Intention requires an ongoing practice rather than a one-time moral commitment. The shadow does not dissolve through a single act of resolution.

## Mindful Decision-Making

The practical application of Right Intention is what might be called mindful decision-making: pausing before action to ask *from what motivation am I about to act?* This pause is itself a form of [[concepts/mindfulness]] applied to the volitional dimension of experience.

Key practice applications:

- **Before responding**: Notice whether the impulse to speak comes from genuine helpfulness, from the need to appear competent, from irritation, or from habit.
- **Before committing**: When setting goals or agreeing to obligations, examine whether the intention is aligned with your actual values or with an image of yourself you are trying to project.
- **In conflict**: Right Intention applied to conflict means checking whether the aim is genuine resolution or the satisfaction of being proved correct.
- **In consumption**: [[concepts/non-attachment]] applied through Right Intention asks whether acquisition is driven by genuine need or by craving for novelty, status, or the reduction of anxiety.

This is not a paralysing practice — it does not require extended deliberation before every action. Over time, the repeated examination of intention reshapes default motivational patterns, so that the pause becomes shorter and the alignment more automatic.

## Relationship to Right Speech

Right Intention and [[concepts/right-speech]] are mutually reinforcing. Right Speech provides the specific constraints on communication (truthfulness, kindness, timeliness, usefulness); Right Intention provides the motivational ground from which those constraints can be consistently maintained.

Speech that is outwardly correct but inwardly condescending, or outwardly kind but inwardly manipulative, fails the standard of Right Intention even if it satisfies the formal criteria of Right Speech. Conversely, genuinely good intention that is expressed clumsily or harmfully calls for development of Right Speech as a practice skill.

The two together produce communication that is both technically sound and authentically motivated — what the Buddhist tradition calls speech that is *helpful and true*.

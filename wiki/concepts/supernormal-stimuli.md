# Supernormal Stimuli

## Overview

Exaggerated versions of natural rewards that hijack the brain's evolved reward system, driving compulsive behavior. Discussed in [[entities/james-clear]]'s *Atomic Habits* (Chapter 8) in the context of the [[concepts/dopamine-driven-feedback-loop]].

## Examples

- **Junk food**: hyper-palatable combinations of salt, sugar, fat that exceed anything found in nature.
- **Social media**: instant likes, variable-ratio reinforcement, endless novelty.
- **Pornography**: bypasses normal social/physical prerequisites for sexual reward.
- **Video games**: engineered challenge curves, variable rewards, status systems.

## Mechanism

- Natural reward systems evolved for a world of scarcity; supernormal stimuli deliver reward signals far beyond what natural cues produce.
- The result is outsized dopamine anticipation → compulsive repetition → difficulty forming habits around subtler natural rewards.

## Implications

- Supernormal stimuli explain why bad habits can feel far more compelling than good ones.
- Designing good habits that compete requires deliberate amplification of their attractiveness (see [[concepts/temptation-bundling]], [[concepts/motivation-rituals]]).

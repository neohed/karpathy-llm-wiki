# Evolved Institutions

## Overview

Institutions, customs, laws, and social practices that have developed through **long historical trial-and-error** rather than deliberate rational design. A central concept across the [[concepts/burke-hayek-popper-triad]].

## Key Points

- [[entities/edmund-burke]]: Prescription — long-established forms embody accumulated intergenerational wisdom.
- [[entities/friedrich-hayek]]: [[concepts/spontaneous-order]] — evolved institutions encode dispersed, tacit knowledge no planner possesses.
- [[entities/karl-popper]]: Skepticism of utopian blueprints — evolved arrangements have been tested and survived; designed replacements have not.
- Their value is partly **invisible**: the knowledge they encode cannot be fully articulated or transferred to a new design.
- Destroying them (even with good intentions) risks losing irreplaceable safeguards against failure, tyranny, and cultural erosion.

## Practical Implication

- Reform burden of proof lies with the reformer, not the defender of existing arrangements.
- Gradualism and reversibility are preconditions for responsible change.

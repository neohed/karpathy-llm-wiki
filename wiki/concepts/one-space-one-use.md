# One Space, One Use

## Overview

A principle from [[entities/james-clear]]'s *Atomic Habits* (Chapter 6) stating that **habits form faster and more reliably when each physical context is associated with a single behavior**. The space itself becomes the cue.

## Key Points

- Contexts accumulate behavioral associations over time; mixing multiple habits in one space dilutes cue strength.
- Examples of the principle in practice:
  - Desk = work only (not entertainment)
  - Bed = sleep only (not scrolling or eating)
  - Specific chair = reading only
- When a single context consistently triggers a single behavior, the environment reliably initiates that behavior without conscious effort.
- Even in limited living spaces, **zones or boundaries** (a specific corner, a particular chair) can preserve contextual clarity.
- Delineated contexts produce more resilient and faster-forming habits than blended ones.

## Relationship to Environment Design

- A specific application of the broader [[concepts/environment-design]] strategy.
- Works by making context the cue — the most durable and automatic type of cue.

# Persona

## Overview

In Jungian analytical psychology, the **persona** is the social mask or role-face that an individual presents to the world — the curated, acceptable self that facilitates social functioning. The term derives from the Latin word for an actor's mask.

## Key Points

- The persona is not the whole self; it is a subset of the psyche adapted to social expectations.
- A very rigid or overbuilt persona typically corresponds to a large, neglected **[[concepts/jungian-shadow]]**.
- [[concepts/shadow-integration]] loosens dependence on the persona — the individual can *choose* to be "the professional" or "the reliable one" without being *imprisoned* by those roles.
- In [[sources/shadow-work]]: personas listed include "the good boy," "the reliable one," "the professional," "the rational thinker."

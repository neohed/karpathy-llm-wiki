# Systems Thinking

## Overview

An analytical approach that models social, historical, or technical phenomena as **complex adaptive systems** — networks of interacting variables, positive/negative feedback loops, and emergent trade-offs — rather than simple cause-and-effect or "goodies vs. baddies" narratives.

## Key Points

- Reframes debates: instead of assigning blame, ask "what variables are interacting and what feedback loops are amplifying outcomes?"
- Practice: for any contested issue, list 5–10 interacting variables, identify positive/negative feedback loops, map trade-offs, and ask "what does this predict over time?"
- Henrich-style extension: ask what selection pressures favored a given cultural norm in a specific historical context.
- Pure **engineering mindset applied to society** — familiar to software and systems engineers.

## Why It's Non-Partisan

- Doesn't prescribe conclusions — it prescribes a richer *map* of causal structure.
- Reveals why simple narratives ("fix X, solve everything") fail: optimizing one variable often degrades others.

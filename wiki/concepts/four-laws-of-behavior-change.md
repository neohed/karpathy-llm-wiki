# Four Laws of Behavior Change

## Overview

A practical design framework from [[entities/james-clear]]'s *Atomic Habits* for building good habits and breaking bad ones. Each law corresponds to one stage of the [[concepts/habit-loop]].

## Building Good Habits

| Law | Habit Loop Stage | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Make it **obvious** | Cue | Surface and design environmental cues |
| 2. Make it **attractive** | Craving | Amplify motivation and desire |
| 3. Make it **easy** | Response | Reduce friction; lower activation energy |
| 4. Make it **satisfying** | Reward | Create immediate positive reinforcement |

## Breaking Bad Habits (Inversion)

| Law | Inversion |
|---|---|
| Make it obvious | Make it **invisible** |
| Make it attractive | Make it **unattractive** |
| Make it easy | Make it **difficult** |
| Make it satisfying | Make it **unsatisfying** |

## Key Points

- Grounded in evolutionary psychology and behavioral science.
- Apply across domains: health, productivity, learning, relationships.
- Each law is both a diagnostic tool (why is a habit failing?) and a design lever (how do I fix it?).

### From [[sources/atomic-habits-part-02]] (2026-05-07)
- **1st Law operationalized (Ch. 5–6)**: Make It Obvious via [[concepts/implementation-intentions]], [[concepts/habit-stacking]], and [[concepts/environment-design]].
- Visual cues are the most powerful category (~10M of ~11M sensory receptors are visual).
- **Inversion of 1st Law (Ch. 7)**: Make It Invisible — remove cues for bad habits. Willpower is unreliable; environmental removal is structural.
- Bad habits are autocatalytic: they create the emotions they are used to cope with, reinforcing a negative loop.
- Highly disciplined people are not resisting temptation more — they are exposed to it less (environment design, not willpower).



### From [[sources/atomic-habits-part-03]] (2026-05-07)
- **2nd Law fully operationalized (Ch. 8–10)**: Make It Attractive via [[concepts/dopamine-driven-feedback-loop]], [[concepts/temptation-bundling]], and social imitation. Inversion: Make It Unattractive via reframing associations and [[concepts/motivation-rituals]].
- **3rd Law fully operationalized (Ch. 11–14)**: Make It Easy via repetition/automaticity, [[concepts/law-of-least-effort]], [[concepts/two-minute-rule]], and environment priming. Inversion: Make It Difficult via [[concepts/commitment-devices]] and automation.
- **4th Law fully operationalized (Ch. 15–17)**: Make It Satisfying via immediate rewards and [[concepts/habit-tracking]]. Inversion: Make It Unsatisfying via [[concepts/accountability-partner]] and [[concepts/habit-contracts]].
- The four laws function as a **continuous diagnostic tool** — revisit and tweak each to remove bottlenecks throughout the habit lifecycle (Conclusion).
- Reward must match the identity being built — conflicting rewards undermine both the habit and the identity vote (Ch. 15).

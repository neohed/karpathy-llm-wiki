# Overton Window

## Overview

The range of ideas and policies considered acceptable or mainstream in public discourse at a given time. Named after Joseph Overton (Mackinac Center for Public Policy). Shifts over time as culture, media, and influential voices push its boundaries.

## Key Points

- Ideas outside the window are deemed radical, taboo, or unthinkable; inside the window they range from acceptable to popular to policy.
- The window can be shifted by sustained advocacy, cultural change, crisis events, or — notably — comedy.
- **Comedy as a window-shifter**: [[entities/jimmy-carr]] argues that humour allows taboo topics to be introduced, examined, and normalised without the defensiveness triggered by earnest political speech. Comedy is a "release valve" and a discursive wedge.

## Relation to Other Concepts

- Related to [[concepts/open-society]]: open societies require mechanisms for expanding acceptable discourse without coercion.
- Related to [[concepts/epistemic-humility]] and [[concepts/steel-manning]]: engaging with uncomfortable ideas is necessary for accurate world-models.

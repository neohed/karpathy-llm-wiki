# Writing as Thinking

## Overview

[[entities/jordan-peterson]]'s core pedagogical insight: **clear writing is clear thinking**. The act of writing forces precision and surfaces the vagueness that casual or ideological language exploits.

## Key Points

- Writing is not a record of thought — it *is* the thought process. You don't know what you think until you write it.
- Rewriting a fuzzy opinion piece into precise prose (defining every loaded term) is a concrete anti-ideological practice.
- Exposes rhetorical sleight-of-hand: terms that feel meaningful in speech ("social justice", "systemic") must be operationalized in writing.
- Connects to [[concepts/epistemic-humility]]: the discipline of defining terms forces acknowledgment of what you don't know.

## Practice

- Take a vague or emotionally loaded source; rewrite it defining every contested term.
- Ask: "What is the falsifiable claim here?" — if none emerges, the writing is ideological rather than analytical.

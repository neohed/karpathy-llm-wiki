# Compassion (Karuna)

## Overview

**Karuna** (Pali/Sanskrit) is one of the four *Brahmaviharas* (Divine Abodes) in Buddhism — the others being loving-kindness (metta), sympathetic joy (mudita), and equanimity (upekkha). Karuna is defined as **recognizing the suffering of others and being moved to alleviate it**.

## Key Points

- Goes beyond empathy: karuna involves not just feeling another's pain but being motivated to act.
- Directed both outward (toward others) and inward (self-compassion).
- Expressed through altruistic action, supportive speech, and non-harm.
- Closely paired with [[concepts/right-intention]] and [[concepts/right-speech]] in ethical practice.

## Application

- Practicing compassion improves relationships and promotes a sense of community.
- Self-compassion (as explored by [[entities/tara-brach]]) reduces self-criticism and supports personal growth.
- Applied to social/environmental issues: compassion motivates action prioritizing collective well-being over personal gain.

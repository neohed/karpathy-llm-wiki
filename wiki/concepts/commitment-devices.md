# Commitment Devices

## Overview

Pre-commitments made in the present that **restrict future choices and lock in beneficial behavior**, from [[entities/james-clear]]'s *Atomic Habits* (Chapter 14). The inversion of the 3rd Law: **Make It Difficult** for bad habits.

## Mechanism

- Shifts the default in favor of good habits by raising the cost or effort of bad ones.
- Leverages the fact that present-self can make binding decisions for future-self when the future-self is more likely to cave to temptation.

## Examples

- Removing junk food from the house; deleting social media apps.
- Prepaying for appointments or gym sessions (painful to cancel).
- Public pledges or bets that create reputational cost for failure.
- Unplugging router after a set hour; using site-blocking software.

## Automation as the Ultimate Commitment Device

- **Automation** is the most powerful form — it shifts effort from repeated willpower decisions to a one-time system decision.
- One-time actions with long-term returns:
  - Health: buy a good mattress, blackout curtains, water filter.
  - Productivity: mute notifications, unsubscribe from email lists, use site blockers.
  - Finance: set up automatic savings, bill pay, and investment contributions.
- Especially useful for infrequent tasks unlikely to become habitual (e.g., rebalancing portfolios, refilling prescriptions).

## Goal

**Design an environment of inevitability** — where the right thing is the path of least resistance and the wrong thing requires significant effort.

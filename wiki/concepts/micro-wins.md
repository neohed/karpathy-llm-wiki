# Micro-Wins

## Overview

The practice of **acknowledging and celebrating small, concrete progress** as a counter to perfectionism, shame, and all-or-nothing thinking. Especially important in trauma recovery and habit formation, where the default internal critic dismisses incomplete progress.

## In Somatic Therapy Context (from [[sources/therapy-grok-survivor-tips]])

- After posting or drafting content, **touch a meaningful object** and say "I showed up."
- Post-task somatic release (sway/stretch + "I took a step, and that's enough") reinforces progress through the body.
- Somatic journaling includes acknowledging what moved, not just what was completed.
- Framed explicitly as **anti-perfectionism**: the goal is presence and action, not flawless output.

## In Habit Science Context (from [[sources/atomic-habits-part-03]])

- Each small action casts an **identity vote** — see [[concepts/identity-based-habits]].
- [[concepts/habit-tracking]] creates visible micro-win records.
- [[concepts/two-minute-rule]] is premised on micro-wins being sufficient to maintain momentum.

## Why It Matters in Trauma Recovery

- [[concepts/shame]] and [[concepts/cptsd]] create a baseline of "nothing is ever enough."
- Micro-wins interrupt this by pairing completion with a positive somatic or tactile signal.
- Builds **body trust** and **self-efficacy** incrementally.

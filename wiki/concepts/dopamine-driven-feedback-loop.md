# Dopamine-Driven Feedback Loop

## Overview

The neurological mechanism underlying habit formation, as described in [[entities/james-clear]]'s *Atomic Habits* (Chapter 8). Habits are powered by dopamine — specifically, by the **anticipation** of reward rather than the reward itself.

## Mechanism

- **Cue → Anticipation (dopamine spike) → Action → Reward → Reinforcement**
- It is the *anticipation* of reward — not the reward itself — that triggers motivation and action.
- Dopamine is released both when anticipating a reward *and* when receiving it, but the anticipatory spike is the primary driver of behavior.

## Wanting vs. Liking

- The brain prioritizes **craving** (wanting) over **enjoyment** (liking).
- The brain's "wanting" circuitry is larger and more influential than its "liking" circuitry.
- This explains why we often chase things more intensely than we enjoy them once obtained.

## Supernormal Stimuli

- Modern environments exploit the dopamine system with **[[concepts/supernormal-stimuli]]**: exaggerated versions of natural rewards (junk food, social media, pornography).
- These hijack the reward system and drive compulsive behavior by delivering unprecedented anticipatory signals.

## Implications for Habit Design

- To make a habit attractive (**2nd Law**): amplify the anticipation associated with it.
- [[concepts/temptation-bundling]] works by borrowing the dopamine anticipation from a desired activity.
- [[concepts/motivation-rituals]] work by conditioning the brain to anticipate a positive state before a habit begins.

# Historicism

## Overview

[[entities/karl-popper]]'s term for the belief that **history has an inevitable direction or goal**, and that this direction can be discerned and used to justify political action. One of his primary targets in *The Open Society and Its Enemies*.

## Key Points

- Historicist thinking underpins totalitarian ideologies: if history is marching toward a predetermined end, those who stand in its way can be crushed.
- Popper's chief examples: Plato (philosopher-kings guided by the Form of the Good), Hegel (the World Spirit unfolding), Marx (historical materialism and the inevitable communist society).
- Historicist blueprints cannot be falsified — they are immune to criticism, making them dangerous.
- Opposed to the experimental, revisable approach of [[concepts/piecemeal-social-engineering]] and the [[concepts/open-society]].

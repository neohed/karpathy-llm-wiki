# Addiction

## Overview

Compulsive engagement with a substance or behavior despite harmful consequences, typically involving disrupted reward pathways and loss of voluntary control.

## In Jungian / Shadow-Work Frame

- [[sources/shadow-work]] frames addiction as **pain that couldn't be voiced** — a symptom of repressed shadow material seeking an outlet.
- One of three primary consequences of shadow suppression, alongside [[concepts/burnout]] and [[concepts/depression]].

## In Behavioral Psychology Frame (Atomic Habits)

- [[sources/atomic-habits-part-03]] frames addiction in terms of the [[concepts/dopamine-driven-feedback-loop]] and [[concepts/supernormal-stimuli]]: hijacked reward circuits offering immediate relief from discomfort.
- The two framings are complementary: the shadow-work account explains the *source* of the pain; the behavioral account explains the *mechanism* of the compulsion.

### From [[sources/therapy-grok-survivor-tips]] (2026-05-07)
- Addiction appears in this source as **binge-viewing** and implied substance use — avoidant behaviors used to escape trauma dysregulation.
- The somatic approach frames cravings as nervous system states to be regulated, not willpower failures to be overcome.
- [[concepts/sobriety]] practices: tactile anchor during cravings, time boundaries, morning reset, redirecting energy into purposeful micro-tasks.
- Parallels [[sources/shadow-work]]'s framing of addiction as repressed shadow pain — both sources treat addiction as a symptom of unprocessed internal material.

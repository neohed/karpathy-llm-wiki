# Shame

## Overview

**Shame** is the painful belief that one is fundamentally defective, worthless, or unacceptable — distinct from guilt ("I did something bad") in that it attacks the self rather than a specific action. A core wound in [[concepts/cptsd]] and trauma recovery.

## Shame in Trauma Recovery (from [[sources/therapy-grok-survivor-tips]])

- Shame reduces body trust and reinforces isolation.
- It is framed as **a trauma artifact**, not truth: *"This is trauma talking, not truth."*
- Somatic shame release: shake body 30 seconds, rest hand on chest, speak the reframe.
- Somatic journaling externalizes shame somatically, reducing its grip.
- [[concepts/micro-wins]] and tactile celebration counter shame's perfectionism trap.

## Shame in Jungian / Shadow Frame (from [[sources/shadow-work]])

- Shame is the mechanism by which the shadow is suppressed — the shadow is rejected because it is considered shameful.
- Integration requires meeting shadow content without shaming it.
- See [[concepts/shadow-integration]].

## Shared Thread Across Sources

- Both somatic therapy and Jungian shadow work converge on shame as the primary obstacle to wholeness and self-expression.
- Both propose **meeting the feeling in the body** (somatic release / shadow dialogue) rather than shaming it further.

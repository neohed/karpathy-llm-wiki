# Habit Stacking

## Overview

A strategy from [[entities/james-clear]]'s *Atomic Habits* (Chapter 5) for building new habits by **chaining them onto existing ones**. An established habit serves as the cue for the new behavior.

## Format

> *"After [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]."*

## Key Points

- Leverages the automaticity of existing habits as reliable cues — no new cue needs to be established from scratch.
- Practical expression of the **1st Law: Make It Obvious** (the existing habit makes the cue obvious).
- Can be extended into multi-habit sequences: "After A, I will do B; after B, I will do C."
- Ties into the [[concepts/diderot-effect]]: one habit naturally triggering a cascade of good habits — routine momentum.
- Cue quality matters: the anchor habit should be consistent in frequency and clearly defined.

## Example

- "After I pour my morning coffee, I will meditate for one minute."
- "After I sit down at my desk, I will open my task list before anything else."
